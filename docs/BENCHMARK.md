# Sentinel Graph Benchmark & Baseline Evaluation

## Controlled Synthetic Benchmark (Pipeline Correctness, Not Real-World Accuracy)

This document records the empirical results of our three-arm benchmark evaluating graph-agentic fraud investigation against traditional score thresholds and bare LLM baselines.

> **Mandatory Real-Data Disclosure:**  
> On the original hackathon round's real dataset (not redistributed), verdict accuracy was 60% (12/20) and pattern accuracy 85% (17/20). The synthetic benchmark checks pipeline correctness, not real-world accuracy.

---

## 1. Three-Arm Evaluation Methodology

To evaluate the contribution of knowledge graph evidence and deterministic policy verification, we compare three distinct arms on the 20-case benchmark pack:

1. **Arm 1: Graph Agent + Deterministic Policy Engine (Full System)**  
   The complete Sentinel Graph pipeline: Graph traversal queries (device sharing, velocity, card-testing sequences, customer case memory) assemble structured evidence; NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Nano-30B proposes a candidate fraud pattern; the deterministic rules engine (R1?R10) decides the final verdict and Next-Best-Action (NBA).
2. **Arm 2: Risk-Score Threshold Only (Traditional ML Triage)**  
   Simulates conventional alert triage by sweeping score thresholds $\tau \in [0.30, 0.85]$ directly on the real-time transaction `risk_score`. Cases with $\text{score} \ge \tau$ are classified as fraud; below $\tau$ as legitimate.
3. **Arm 3: Plain Nemotron Nano (No Graph Evidence Ablation)**  
   Ablation arm querying NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B via Nebius Token Factory with a flat list of recent card transactions (amounts, timestamps, channels), explicitly stripped of graph topology, device-linkage counts, and connected card history.

## 3. Constructed Stress Scenarios Explaining Baseline Separation

The synthetic benchmark incorporates two specific **constructed stress scenarios** designed to test edge cases where scalar risk scores fundamentally fail:

### Stress Scenario 1: `CASE-009` ? Card-Testing Ring With a Low-Score Cash-Out (False Negative under ML Score)
- **Constructed Profile**: Three micro-authorizations ($1.79, $2.29, $4.93) on one card inside six hours, then a $2,093.43 online charge on that same card. The real-time model scores the charge **0.48** and the micro-authorizations 0.70.
- **Why Arm 2 Fails**: Any threshold $\tau > 0.48$ (including the optimal $\tau = 0.70$) marks the charge legitimate. This is a severe False Negative: the ring's probe payments score high, but the cash-out that follows them does not.
- **Why Arm 3 Fails**: Without graph context, plain Nemotron sees one isolated charge and none of the ring; it cannot connect the charge to the three probes or to the 17 other cards carrying the same device profile.
- **How Arm 1 Resolves It**: Device-profile traversal shows the profile shared across 17 other cards, with 4 confirmed-fraud cards in a ring of 18. The policy engine assesses $P(\text{fraud}) \approx 0.81$, keeps the pattern `undocumented` under R9, queues block-card and monitor-connected-cards, and files the SAR under R2 (confirmed fraud, exposure > $1,000).

### Stress Scenario 2: `CASE-007` ? Legitimate Emergency Medical Purchase (False Positive under ML Score)
- **Constructed Profile**: Large out-of-state hospital transaction ($1,450.00). Due to high dollar amount and geographic anomaly, the raw risk score spikes to **0.86**.
- **Why Arm 2 Fails**: Pure thresholding flags this transaction as fraud, blocking an urgent cardholder medical transaction.
- **How Arm 1 Resolves It**: The customer verification channel confirms a verified travel notification and matched 3DS biometric token. Deterministic policy rule R1 overrides the raw risk score down to $P(\text{fraud}) = 0.12$ (`BENIGN`), allowing the payment and sending a notification.

## 4. Reproducing the Benchmark

To reproduce the exact numbers reported above:

```bash
# 1. Regenerate synthetic CSVs and SQLite database
python scripts/make_synthetic_data.py
python -m backend.scripts.prepare_data

# 2. Run the 3-arm comparison
python scripts/compare_baselines.py
```

The script outputs `artifacts/compare_baselines.json` with per-case decisions, latencies, and token counts.
