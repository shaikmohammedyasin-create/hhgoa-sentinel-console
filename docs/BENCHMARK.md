# Sentinel Graph Benchmark & Baseline Evaluation

## Controlled Synthetic Benchmark (Pipeline Correctness, Not Real-World Accuracy)

This document records the empirical results of our three-arm benchmark evaluating graph-agentic fraud investigation against traditional score thresholds and bare LLM baselines.

> **Mandatory Data Disclosure:**  
> On the original hackathon round's real dataset (not redistributed), verdict accuracy was 60% (12/20) and pattern accuracy 85% (17/20). The synthetic benchmark checks pipeline correctness, not real-world accuracy.

---

## 1. Three-Arm Evaluation Methodology

To evaluate the contribution of knowledge graph evidence and deterministic policy verification, we compare three distinct arms on the 20-case benchmark pack:

1. **Arm 1: Graph Agent + Deterministic Policy Engine (Full System)**  
   The complete Sentinel Graph pipeline: Graph traversal queries (device sharing, velocity, card-testing sequences, customer case memory) assemble structured evidence; NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Nano-30B proposes a fraud pattern; the deterministic rules engine (R1?R10) decides the final verdict and Next-Best-Action (NBA).
2. **Arm 2: Risk-Score Threshold Only (Traditional ML Triage)**  
   Simulates conventional alert triage by sweeping score thresholds $	au \in [0.30, 0.85]$ directly on the real-time transaction `risk_score`. Cases with $	ext{score} \ge 	au$ are classified as fraud; below $	au$ as legitimate.
3. **Arm 3: Plain Nemotron Nano (No Graph Evidence)**  
   Ablation arm querying NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B via Nebius Token Factory with a flat list of recent card transactions (amounts, timestamps, channels), explicitly stripped of graph topology, device-linkage counts, and connected card history.

## 3. Analysis: Why Graph Intelligence Beats Score Triage

A naive synthetic pack where legitimate cases score $\le 0.67$ and fraud cases score $\ge 0.84$ allows a trivial score threshold to reach $F_1 = 1.0$ by construction. To test true pipeline capabilities, our generator plants two real-world stress scenarios with overlapping risk distributions:

### Stress Scenario (a): Coordinated Micro-Payment Ring (`CASE-009`)
* **Behavior:** A syndicated fraud ring conducting small authorizations ($14.50) across multiple cards on a shared device cluster.
* **Risk Score:** Scored at **0.48** by the real-time model (appears low risk).
* **Score-Only Failure:** At the optimal threshold ($	au = 0.70$), Arm 2 misses this ring entirely (**False Negative**).
* **Graph Agent Success:** Graph traversal traces the device fingerprint, identifies 17 connected cards with prior confirmed card-testing cases, triggers Rule R9/R10 (`undocumented` coordinated fraud), and blocks the ring.

### Stress Scenario (b): Legitimate High-Score Emergency Purchase (`CASE-007`)
* **Behavior:** A customer making an urgent medical/hospital or travel purchase ($1,450.00) in an atypical category.
* **Risk Score:** Scored at **0.86** by the real-time model (triggering high-risk alerts).
* **Score-Only Failure:** Arm 2 flags and freezes the card (**False Positive**), blocking a valid emergency transaction.
* **Graph Agent Success:** The agent reviews customer graph history (zero prior fraud), requests customer validation, verifies the cardholder's authorization, and executes Rule R1 (`CLOSE_NO_FRAUD`), clearing the transaction.

### Scope and Limitations
The stress scenarios show a specific failure mode of score-only triage on topology-dependent patterns (syndicates, emergency anomalies), not general superiority across arbitrary unstructured payment feeds. Graph traversal adds significant investigative accuracy where entities share underlying hardware or account clusters, but incurs latency overhead compared to pure sub-millisecond scoring.

## 4. Live Nemotron Token Factory Measurements

Live calls were executed against the Nebius Token Factory endpoint (`https://api.tokenfactory.nebius.com/v1`) using the 3-tier architecture:

| Role | Tier | Target Model | Measured Latency (ms) | Tokens (In / Out) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Role 1: Pattern Classifier** | Nano | `nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B` | 8,322.2 ms | 592 / 613 | Verified (`card_testing`, conf 0.85) |
| **Role 2: Verification Drafter** | Nano | `nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B` | 6,051.8 ms | 428 / 568 | Verified (137 chars customer draft) |
| **Role 3: Narrative & SAR Draft** | Ultra | `nvidia/Nemotron-3-Ultra-550b-a55b` | 4,339.8 ms | 594 / 919 | Verified (533 chars summary + 1157 chars SAR) |
| **Role 4: Analyst Q&A** | Ultra | `nvidia/Nemotron-3-Ultra-550b-a55b` | 1,295.4 ms | 649 / 100 | Verified (grounded Q&A response) |

### Nano vs Super Live Classifier Comparison
On identical input evidence (`EV-01`, `EV-02`):
* **Nano (`nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B`)**: 592 prompt tokens, 503 completion tokens, 6,778.9 ms, proposed `card_testing` (conf 0.85).
* **Super (`nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b`)**: 592 prompt tokens, 453 completion tokens, 3,184.8 ms, proposed `card_testing` (conf 0.85).
* Both models produced 100% valid JSON adhering strictly to the schema. Nano was selected for high-volume automated triage due to optimal cost efficiency, with Super serving as fallback.
